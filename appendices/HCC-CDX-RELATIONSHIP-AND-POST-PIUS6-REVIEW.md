## 1. Purpose

Preserve a clear, reciprocal agreement-in-proposal about the relationship between CDX and the Human Canon Charta (HCC), including what the two bodies must communicate, how they retain separate authority and source provenance, and what must be examined once Pius 6 is ready.

This note is written now **in CDX** because the question concerns estate-wide governance requirements. It does not edit the current HCC, pre-empt Pius 6 acceptance, amend the Conductellence Act, or pronounce existing ethical/legal assurance complete.

The future CDX-owned **LACE** concept (Legal Audit of Conduct for Excellence; “do a LACE”) is acknowledged as a proposed command-invoked audit procedure. LACE is **not** a standalone system, repository authority, constitutional body, or currently registered operational command. If developed, it uses CDX's governing machinery and HCC's traceable doctrine, under CDX's own change-control and runtime controls.

## 2. Existing source and authority boundaries

| Body | Canonical source and scope | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applicable external law and fundamental rights | Authoritative legal sources, interpreted for actual jurisdiction, facts, roles and effective date | Superior to internal CDX and HCC instruments; neither internal doctrine establishes legal applicability alone |
| CDX | Conductellence Act CDX-LAW-001 v1.0.0 and CDX governance, controls, legal resolver, runtime, review and evidence arrangements | Estate-wide constitutional and operational governance for delegated non-human digital activity; CDX law is internally authoritative |
| HCC | Human Canon Charta HCC-S1-V1.0, source within Pius System Codex 5.0 Part 5 | Distinct moral and human-consequence doctrine. It is canonically admitted into CDX as Appendix A by reference and traceable source provenance, not by copying every clause into CDX law |
| Ex-Inerta Libertad | Historical Pius source doctrine, separately admitted as CDX Appendix B | Ontological boundary on artificial self-sovereignty, distinct from HCC's consequence/ethical function |
| Pius 6 | Hybrismannen/pius-universe, separate product acceptance/release boundary | Future review input only once an explicit accepted source baseline exists; development artefacts are not silently treated as a released Codex |
| FFOS | Motherpher/FFOS | May surface, coordinate or invoke CDX-owned procedures through explicit versioned bindings; FFOS does not own CDX constitutional source or HCC doctrine |

Two different directions must be maintained:

1. **Source/doctrinal provenance:** HCC originates in Pius, and CDX incorporates the source doctrine by versioned reference.
2. **Estate operational governance:** CDX governs non-human execution in declared governed surfaces. HCC contributes moral/consequence constraints, and a valid HCC restriction cannot originate new legal authority or expand capability.

“Everything important in HCC is accessible to CDX” means **complete source linkage, applicability and operational traceability**, not indiscriminate duplication, annexation, or independent rewriting of the original doctrine.

## 3. What CDX requires from a future reviewed HCC

The post-Pius-6 review should determine whether HCC can supply CDX with sufficiently explicit, reviewable answers to the following, without pretending ethical judgments are deterministic legal findings:

| CDX requirement | Expected HCC review question or interface | Evidence expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moral foundations and rights sensitivity | Are dignity, justice, equality, non-discrimination, participation, autonomy, ecological integrity and affected-person interests meaningfully addressed? How do moral propositions differ from binding legal rights? | Versioned doctrine clauses; rights/ethics mapping; limitations and conflicts |
| Human-consequence analysis | Can HCC describe direct, indirect, structural, cumulative, delayed and unequal harms, including the people not represented in datasets? | Affected-person/community/ecology scope; uncertainty and missing representation; consequence rationale |
| Plurality and disagreement | How are competing values, moral uncertainty, culturally/contextually different perspectives, and legitimate disagreement represented without flattening them to a single model confidence score? | Reasoned alternatives, unresolved conflicts, competent review and escalation |
| Independent ethical oversight | Who may issue ethical clearance, restrictions, degraded state or stop; and how is the reviewer independent from the actor and beneficiary of the decision? | Attributable reviewer/authority, version, scope, conflict-of-interest control and review outcome |
| Decision interface | Can the existing HCC_CLEAR, HCC_CLEAR_WITH_CONTROLS, HCC_REVIEW_REQUIRED, HCC_DEGRADED, HCC_STOP grammar be interpreted consistently and mapped without implying legal authorization? | Versioned semantics, required inputs/outputs, restrictions, uncertainty and revocation |
| Correctability and remedy | Can affected parties' objections, new harm evidence, contestation, correction and post-decision learning change future assessments through human-governed review? | Review/appeal routes appropriate to the context, outcome and provenance; historic evidence preserved |
| Audit and traceability | Can each consequential HCC assessment be reconstructed and related to an exact CDX effect, legal review, version and SPETL event? | Object/action IDs, HCC and CDX pins, mandate, consequence domains, references and closure state |
| Ethical drift and maintenance | How is changing evidence, human-rights understanding or social consequence examined without unauthorized automatic doctrinal mutation? | Versioned review schedule, trigger conditions, source quality and accepted change record |

These are **future requirements for assessment**, not assertions that HCC presently lacks all these functions. A gap may be doctrinal, interpretive, procedural, evidentiary or purely an implementation gap. The review must classify it before recommending a change.

## 4. What HCC requires from CDX

The relationship is reciprocal. CDX must give HCC a legitimate operational context in which its ethical contributions can matter:

| HCC dependency on CDX | CDX responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Legal versus ethical separation | Supply a source-verified legal applicability conclusion or the honest states UNKNOWN / LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED; ethical clearance cannot substitute for lawfulness |
| Identity, authority and scope | Supply authenticated operating principal, mandate, relevant decision owner, attempted effect, target, context and consequences; a model or retrieved text cannot create authority |
| Versioned interface and source preservation | Maintain precise HCC source/version/digest and CDX law/policy pins; keep doctrinal claims separate from derived interpretation and operational policy |
| Operational restriction | Provide a bounded, fail-closed means to apply properly authorized HCC stop/review/control decisions within affected consumer systems; no permission amplification |
| Evidence and audit | Preserve HCC consequence findings with SPETL references, independent verification and historical reconstruction; do not let an executing agent accept its own ethics |
| Feedback from practice | Return structured incident, complaint, counterevidence, representation-gap, drift, override and remediation findings to the HCC review process |
| Human decision rights | Route genuinely legal, ethical or constitutional disputes to accountable qualified human review and prevent silent automated adjudication |
| Proportionality and usability | Differentiate routine low-consequence controls from cases demanding deep ethical review, without bypassing non-waivable prohibitions |

A failure to establish legally material facts must not become a presumed legal PASS. An HCC_STOP must not be treated as a grant of authority; it is restrictive within its proper scope.

## 5. Operational relationship: CDX, HCC and proposed LACE

The intended interaction is:

1. A CDX-governed request or audit trigger identifies its actor, mandate, target, context, consequence and governing version.
2. CDX identifies applicable internal controls and the legal source/applicability position. Material ambiguity routes to legal review; it is not decided by HCC or by model confidence.
3. Where the human-consequence dimension is material, CDX resolves the applicable versioned HCC doctrine and an authorized ethical review/evaluation path.
4. CDX retains distinguishable **legal**, **ethical**, **technical-policy** and **evidence** findings. Disagreement is preserved, not averaged into an opaque score.
5. Only the authorized CDX/local effect gate may decide whether a consequential digital operation proceeds under governing law, mandate, restrictions and current trust state.
6. SPETL and authorized review capture decision, actual effect, unresolved issues and feedback to the relevant doctrine/control owner.

**LACE (proposed)** would be a more comprehensive named procedure invoking this machinery for a specific legal/ethical conduct audit. It remains within CDX: no separate CDX anchor or constitutional identity is created by the procedure. A future LACE-supporting legal agent is a distinct AMA-governed implementation subject with its own bounded mandate, admission and local CDX enforcement; invoking LACE does not grant the agent legal or constitutional authority.

Nothing here establishes a LACE command, workflow ID, production endpoint, permission or automatic audit cadence. These require later design, verification and separate approval.

## 6. Post-Pius-6 review sequencing and gates

**Gate 0 — Preserve current law and HCC:** Until a Pius 6 product is explicitly accepted, continue to reference the currently admitted HCC-S1-V1.0 source doctrine with its provenance. Do not infer that unaccepted Pius 6 proposals supersede it.

**Gate 1 — Source eligibility:** Verify the precise Pius 6 accepted product/version/commit, approved HCC source, review authority, source hashes and lineage from the historical HCC. If no accepted relevant HCC source exists, retain Gate 0 and document the blocker.

**Gate 2 — Bilateral gap review:** Review both the HCC→CDX and CDX→HCC tables in sections 3 and 4. Test human-rights coverage, ethics, applicability, evidence, disputed cases, conflicts, duties of review, operational consequence and proportionality. Distinguish doctrinal omission from unimplemented procedure.

**Gate 3 — Source-specific reconciliation:** Produce a source-to-source crosswalk: historical HCC → accepted Pius 6/HCC → CDX Appendix A → CDX interpretation/control/review/evidence contract. For every change, record normative meaning, compatibility, temporal effect, governance owner, affected consumer and migration requirement.

**Gate 4 — Independent substantive review:** Seek legally qualified review for material legal questions and meaningful human ethical review for non-deterministic moral judgment; consider affected people and adverse evidence. Neither an AI agent nor an automated compliance test may self-adopt moral doctrine.

**Gate 5 — Separate authorization:** Under the relevant Pius and CDX change-control processes, decide whether to (a) retain present HCC doctrine, (b) accept a new HCC source version, (c) revise only CDX interpretation/operational mappings, or (d) propose an actual constitutional amendment. Each action requires its own evidence, version and owner approval. **No automatic promotion or silent amendment.**

**Gate 6 — Operational conformance:** Where admitted changes affect CDX/LACE/agent behavior, verify exact version pins, permitted/restricted cases, legal-review stops, HCC restrictions, human decision paths, provenance and actual effect-gate enforcement. Do not infer live enforcement from documents, anchors or static schema tests.

## 7. Review deliverables to request after Gate 1

The authorized review should return:

- a version-pinned **HCC–CDX Source and Authority Crosswalk**;
- a **Human Rights and Ethical Adequacy Assessment** distinguishing enforceable legal obligations from moral doctrine and identifying gaps/uncertainties;
- a **Bidirectional Interface and Evidence Matrix** for CDX↔HCC (inputs, findings, stops, review and SPETL links);
- a **LACE Integration Requirements Note**, if LACE has by then been separately approved for design, with no automatic runtime authorization;
- a **Change/No-Change Decision and Migration Ledger** with owners, reasons, reservations, tests and applicable versions.

Each item is a proposed future output, not a presently issued acceptance certificate.
